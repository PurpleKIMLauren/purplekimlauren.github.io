## What the shape shows

The war escalates sharply from 2015, when a Saudi-led coalition intervened against Houthi forces,
and peaks in 2021 at roughly 23,000 battle-related deaths — the deadliest year in the series.
After the 2022 truce, recorded battle deaths fall steeply, to around 1,000–1,700 a year.

## Why this is a floor, not a ceiling

UCDP counts **battle-related deaths** — people killed in fighting between organized actors. It does
not count the war’s indirect toll: deaths from collapsed health systems, hunger, and preventable
disease, which in Yemen are widely estimated to be far larger than the direct toll. Read this chart
as the hard, conservative floor of the human cost, not the full picture.

It is also worth saying what a single national total hides. A yearly number tells you nothing about
who was killed, where, or by which weapon — the questions that matter most for civilian protection.
Future posts here work down to that level.

## Method and sources

- **Data:** UCDP Georeferenced Event Dataset, aggregated to country-year, best estimate.
- **Access:** downloaded as CSV via Our World in Data (redistributable, UCDP is CC-BY). No account needed.
